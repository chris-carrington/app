## Why are we Shasta Trades Trust + Shasta Trades Nonprofit
- The Trust + Nonprofit combination permanently protects our mission from being changed, sold, or corrupted
    - A nonprofit mission can be changed w/ a Board vote
    - A Trusts mission cannot legally change unless it becomes "illegal, impossible, or impracticable" to carry out. Even then, we need court approval and must notify the Attorney General.
    - The Trust is the member (owner) of the Nonprofit, the ultimate decision maker for the nonprofit & ensures every nonprofit decision is mission focused
- An LLC, S-Corporation or C-Corporation is profit focused
    - Created to make money
- A nonprofit is mission focused
    - Created to make a difference

---

## What is a Trust Document?
- Legal contract that brings the Trust into existence
- Reviewed by the states Attorney General
- Defines the Trusts mission
    - Ensure the mission is protected forever
    - We cannot change the Trust's mission unless it becomes "illegal, impossible, or impracticable" to carry out
- Names the Trustee(s) & tells them exactly how they must operate

---

## What is a Charitable Trust?
- The Charitable Trust is a nonprofit's member (owner)
- A standard nonprofit has a person’s name as the member. Our format is Shasta Trades Trust is the member of Shasta Trades Nonprofit
- Shasta Trades Trust has a mission
    - We cannot change the Trust's mission unless it becomes "illegal, impossible, or impracticable" to carry out
- Shasta Trades Nonprofit has a mission
    - A nonprofit mission can be changed w/ a Board vote
- Our Trust mission is nearly identical to our nonprofit mission
    - The trust mission is more broad (any community) than the nonprofit mission (Mt Shasta community)
    - This way if we ever want to expand (Yreka, Dusmuir, Redding, etc.,), we change the nonprofit mission w/ a Board vote and the Trust mission remains the same
- Shasta Trades Trust has 2 Trustees who can veto any nonprofit idea (e.g., change nonprofit mission, hire someone, alter a salary, start working on a new project) that does not align w/ our Trusts mission
- If a Trustee violates the Trust’s mission, California’s Attorney General will prosecute them
- The nonprofit does the charitable work and the trust protects the mission forever

---

## What is a Trustee?
- Has a strict legal duty to act in the best interest of the Trust's mission
- Must avoid conflicts of interest
- Cannot use Trust assets (nonprofit) for their own personal gain
- Must follow the Trust Document exactly as it is written
- Must keep beneficiaries (public) informed
- Must be open and transparent about how the Trust is being managed
- Must respond to reasonable requests from the public for information about the Trust
    - If someone asks about the Trust's purpose, assets, or activities, the Trustee must provide a clear answer
- When the Trust receives its first asset (the Nonprofit), the Trustee must register with the Attorney General's Registry of Charitable Trusts within 30 days by providing:
    - Form CT-1 (Initial Registration Form)
    - A copy of the Trust Document
    - A registration fee of $50
- Must file an annual report (Form RRF-1) with the Attorney General's Registry of Charitable Trusts every year, due 5 months after Shasta Trades fiscal year ends
- Must file the annual IRS Form 990 tax return
