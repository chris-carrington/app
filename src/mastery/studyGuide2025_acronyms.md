# Section: Acronyms


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What is CSLB?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Contractors State Licensing Board
- The government agency responsible for regulating California’s construction industry
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  Does CSLB license people?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- No, only business entities
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What are the contractor classifications?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Class A
    - General Engineering Contractors
    - Requires specialized engineering
    - Can build examples: freeways, skyscrapers, dams
- Class B
    - General Building Contractors
    - May accept jobs that require framing, carpentry or at least two other building trades
- B-2
    - Residential Remodeling Classification
    - May not:
      - Make structural alterations to load bearing partitions and walls
      - Install or extend electrical or plumbing systems
      - Install or replace HVAC systems
- Class C
    - Specialty Contractors
    - Examples:
        - Electrical
        - HVAC
        - Landscaping
        - Plumbing
        - Roofing
        - Solar
        - Swimming Pools
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What is ICC?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- The International Code Council
- Not an international organization
- A non-profit organization that creates and maintains the I-Codes
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What are I-Codes?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Construction model codes created by the ICC
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What are model codes?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- A template (model) for a law
- Does not have the force of law on its own
- A jurisdiction must officially adopt and potentially amend a model code for it to become legally binding w/in its borders
- With the I-Codes (model codes) as a foundation, the BSC updates and adopts a new edition of Title 24 (state regulations) every three years through a process called the Triennial Code Adoption Cycle
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What is the CBSC or BSC?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- The California Building Standards Commission
- With the I-Codes (model codes) as a foundation, the BSC updates and adopts a new edition of Title 24 (state regulations) every three years through a process called the Triennial Code Adoption Cycle
- After this formal process, the updated Title 24 becomes law across the entire state of California
- A California city cannot make the rules less strict than Title 24 but they can add their own stricter requirements
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What is CCR?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- The California Code of Regulations
- The official compilation of all state agency regulations in California
- Organized into 28 Titles
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What is a Title (CCR Perspective)?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- The broadest category of subject matter w/in the CCR
- Each Title covers a major area of regulation
- Examples:
    - Title 5 = Education
    - Title 24 = California Building Standards Code
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What is Title 24?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- Title 24 of the CCR
- California Building Standards Code (the official name for Title 24)
- State regulation
- Legally binding
- Adopts the I-Codes as its foundation and then adds California specific amendments
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What is the California Building Standards Code?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- The official name for Title 24 of the CCR
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What is the IBC?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- The International Building Code
- One of the 15 model codes w/in the I-Codes
- Establishes minimum requirements to safeguard public health, safety and welfare in new and existing buildings and structures
- Primarily focus on structures not covered by the IRC (e.g., commercial buildings, apartments, dormitories)
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What is the IRC?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- The International Residential Code
- One of the 15 model codes w/in the I-Codes
- Establishes minimum requirement for one and two family dwellings and townhouses (residential construction)
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What is the CBC?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- The California Building Code
- Part 2 of Title 24
- Based on the IBC w/ California specific amendments
- California’s legally binding version of the IBC
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->


<!--{"accordionStart":true}-->
##  What is the CRC?
<!--{"accordionBody":true}-->
- The California Residential Code
- Part 2.5 of Title 24
- Based on the IRC w/ California specific amendments
- California’s legally binding version of the IRC
<!--{"accordionEnd":true}-->
